# El Niño, La Niña, and Monsoon Intensity

El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) refers to periodic warming (El Niño) and
cooling (La Niña) of central and eastern Pacific Ocean surface temperatures.
These phases influence global atmospheric circulation patterns, including the
strength of the Indian Southwest Monsoon.

El Niño years are statistically associated with below-average Indian monsoon
rainfall, as the warming disrupts the pressure gradients that drive moisture
transport toward the subcontinent. Conversely, La Niña years often correlate
with above-average or normal monsoon rainfall, as cooler Pacific temperatures
tend to strengthen the monsoon circulation.

However, this relationship is not absolute. Other factors, such as the Indian
Ocean Dipole (IOD), can offset or amplify ENSO's influence. A positive IOD
phase, for instance, can partially counteract the suppressive effect of an
El Niño event on Indian rainfall.
