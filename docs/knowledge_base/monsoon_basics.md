# Southwest Monsoon Basics

The Southwest Monsoon, also called the Indian Summer Monsoon, is a seasonal wind
reversal that brings the majority of India's annual rainfall between June and
September. It originates from differential heating between the Indian landmass
and the surrounding Indian Ocean during summer, causing moisture-laden winds to
flow from the ocean toward the subcontinent.

The monsoon typically makes landfall over the Kerala coast around the first
week of June and progressively advances northward and eastward, covering the
entire country by mid-July. Its withdrawal begins from northwest India in
September and completes by early October.

Monsoon onset and withdrawal dates vary year to year, influenced by factors
such as sea surface temperatures, the Indian Ocean Dipole, and El Niño-Southern
Oscillation (ENSO) conditions. Early or delayed onset can significantly affect
agricultural planning, particularly for kharif crop sowing.
